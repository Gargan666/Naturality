PIXELATED TERRAIN LIGHTING
-------------------------
Terrain ambient occlusion, block light and sky light are sampled at the center of fixed 1/16-block cells,
matching the grid of standard 16x16 block textures. The grid is anchored to
section-local block positions, so it stays aligned across blocks, chunk borders,
negative coordinates and camera movement. Slabs/stairs retain the same pixel size.
Vanilla vertex shading/tint and the lightmap are quantized on the same grid
before multiplication; texture sampling, alpha, fog and portal emissivity remain.
AO/vertex shading uses 0.5% brightness increments (NATURALITY_AO_STEPS = 200),
while block/sky light uses adaptive, exponentially spaced brightness levels.
The default endpoint settings give approximately 2% gaps near black,
6% at mid brightness and 10% near white. Exact black and full brightness remain.
The mapping is monotonic: brighter input never produces a darker output.
Each field is quantized independently before multiplication, so the coarser light
bands do not erase AO's finer changes. Vertex shading includes vanilla face shade
and tint. RGB ratios are preserved by scaling from the largest lighting channel.
Texture colors are not posterized. Only lightmap values below roughly 1%
round to black.
Darkness progressively boosts the lit texture's saturation by up to 100%, using
distance from luminance grey. A smoothstep ease begins below 30% combined
lighting and reaches the full boost at 10%; it stays full below that threshold.
Neutral greys and full-bright colors remain unchanged. RGB is display-clamped.
The spatial grid stays at 16x16.

LIGHTING CONTROLS: Mods > Naturality > Configure > Lighting.
Six sliders save to the lighting object in config/naturality.json (run/config
during development). Closing the screen reloads resources if lighting changed.
Without Mod Menu, edit the JSON with the game closed. Values are percentages:
  "lighting": {
    "darkStepPercent": 2.0,
    "lightStepPercent": 10.0,
    "aoStepPercent": 0.5,
    "darkSaturationPercent": 100.0,
    "saturationStartPercent": 30.0,
    "saturationFullPercent": 10.0
  }
Dark/bright steps accept 0.1-10%; bright cannot be below dark. Equal endpoints
give uniform bands. AO accepts 0.1-5%. Saturation boost accepts 0-100%, with 0
disabling it. Start accepts 1-100%; full accepts 0-99% and must be at least 1%
below start. Invalid combinations are clamped on save/load. Missing settings
use defaults, preserving existing portal preferences. Endpoint step sizes are
approximate because the curve uses a whole number of intervals between 0 and 1.
LightingShaderSettings supplies validated constants through
LightingShaderSettingsMixin when pixel_lighting_settings.glsl is loaded.
Directional face shading and full-bright emission are already constant on faces.
Minecraft's ambient occlusion setting must be enabled to see AO; block and sky
light pixelation also apply with AO disabled. All vanilla block light sources use
this path, including torches, lanterns, glowstone and lava lighting nearby terrain.
No extra block geometry or chunk rebuild processing is added.

Implementation: src/client/resources/assets/minecraft/shaders/core/terrain.vsh
and terrain.fsh, plus assets/naturality/shaders/include/pixel_lighting.glsl.
These terrain shader overrides target Minecraft 26.3 and must be compared with
vanilla on version upgrades. Resource packs replacing these shaders, or alternate
renderers/shader packs, can override/bypass this effect. Items/entities are outside
the terrain shader. Rotated model faces use their dominant block-axis plane;
custom UV layouts and higher-resolution textures do not change the fixed grid.
Verification: gradlew assemble; gradlew runClientGameTest -PclientTests=lighting. PixelAmbientOcclusionGameTest
renders a corner scene with solid, cutout and translucent blocks plus slabs/stairs
from two angles, plus roof-edge skylight and nighttime torch gradients;

DYNAMIC LIGHTING
----------------
Mods > Naturality > Configure > Dynamic Lighting controls moving client lights.
Defaults: enabled, 100 ms update interval (2 ticks at 20 TPS), 100% brightness,
48-block source tracking range, 64 nearest sources. Held items (both hands,
including mobs), glowing entities, burning entities and dropped items each have
an independent toggle. All ten controls have Reset and apply without reloading.
Intervals range from 50-1000 ms; shorter intervals cost more light propagation
and chunk rebuild work. Tracking range is not the light radius: vanilla light
levels still fall off by at least one per block, up to level 15. Brightness can be
scaled from 25-200%, clamped to level 15. Multiple emitters use the strongest
light at each cell, not additive over-brightening.

DynamicLighting samples loaded entities near the camera and estimates left/right
hand positions from eye height and facing. Sub-block source positions defaults ON
(dynamicLighting.subBlockPrecision). It retains fractional world positions and
seeds up to eight neighboring light samples by distance to their centers. A
separate field propagates at 1/16-light-level precision with vanilla opacity and
face-occlusion rules. DynamicLightCoordsMixin preserves the fractional values in
rendered lightmap coordinates, including AO blending. The existing 16x16 surface
pixel grid and configured brightness bands still apply. Turning the option OFF
restores the original block-snapped mode. This is not skeletal hand tracking or
per-frame interpolation; the chosen update interval controls movement refreshes.
Source membership and brightness are checked each tick for immediate handoffs.
Torches, lanterns and luminous
block items use their default block emission. Lava buckets, glow berries, blaze
rods/powder and glowstone dust/ink sacs also emit. Entity levels are listed in
dynamicLighting.entityLightLevels in config/naturality.json; modded IDs can be
added and a zero value disables a particular entity. Vanilla defaults include
blazes, glow squid, magma cubes, allays, vexes, wardens, glow item frames,
creakings, emissive eyes and fireballs. Charged creepers emit level 15; glow squid
temporarily stop emitting during their damage-induced dark state. Emissive
textures/outline effects alone are not a reliable light-source registry.

DynamicBlockLightMixin augments getEmission only for the exact current client
block-light engine. Minecraft propagates that light through air and around
occluding blocks, updates terrain meshes and supplies entity light samples. It
uses the existing pixelated terrain light shader. In sub-block mode this engine
receives the integer portion of the fractional seeds; the precision field adds
the remaining rendered brightness. Fractional changes explicitly invalidate
affected mesh sections even when integer light is unchanged. The precision field
is cached between source/geometry changes, with periodic refreshes. This costs
additional CPU and chunk rebuilding; use a longer interval or fewer sources if
needed. No blocks are placed, server
lighting is untouched, and gameplay/spawning does not change. State is replaced
atomically; old source cells are checked when sources move, disappear or are
disabled. Dimension changes clear the snapshot. Client-only lights are never
written to the server world. LightSourcePlacement sweeps hand emitters against
collision shapes and pushes embedded body sources toward a nearby exposed face.
DynamicLightStorageMixin overlays the client field on light queries even where
vanilla has no section storage. DynamicLightDropMixin bridges local throws while
the server's dropped-item spawn is pending (bounded to 20 ticks).

Validation: gradlew assemble runClientGameTest -PclientTests=dynamic-lighting.
The focused suite covers menu resets, both hands, movement cleanup, wall blocking,
within-block movement, continuity across cell boundaries, precision-mode toggling,
server isolation, disabling/re-enabling, blaze/glow-squid light and dropped items.

PIXEL FOG PROTOTYPE
-------------------
The shared vanilla fog include now samples fog distance on a 4x4 framebuffer
pixel grid and uses eight density intervals with a fixed 4x4 ordered-dither
pattern. Nearby haze below 40% fog density remains smooth, without distance
snapping or dithering. Pixelation eases in from 40% to 75% density and stays at
full strength toward the dense fog boundary. This affects fog only, retaining original
texture detail, fog color, opacity endpoints and vanilla start/end distances.
Unlike terrain lighting, this is a screen-space grid, not block-texture alignment.
Distance at cell centers is estimated from each surface's fragment derivatives;
no neighboring depth is read. Perspective/triangle boundaries can still produce
small variations, so this is an art-direction prototype, not a low-resolution
volumetric simulation. Screen size affects its apparent pixel size.

Scope: shaders using vanilla linear_fog_value/total_fog_value/apply_fog, including
terrain, entities, particles, water/lava/Nether/status-effect distance fog, cloud
fading, glint and lightning fades. It does not pixelate sky textures, weather
textures, fullscreen fluid overlays or custom fog implementations in shader packs.
PixelFogShaderMixin marks fragment sources so fog.glsl remains valid in vertex
shaders. Mods > Naturality > Configure > Fog has Custom fog (default ON) and
Fog pixel size (1x1 to 32x32 screen pixels, default 4x4), with individual resets.
Smaller pixels give finer resolution. OFF fully restores vanilla fog, including
colored End fog, without changing the terrain lighting settings. Changes apply
on leaving the page via resource reload. JSON: "fog": { "enabled": true,
"pixelSize": 4 } in config/naturality.json. Missing settings use defaults;
out-of-range sizes clamp to 1-32. FogShaderSettings generates the shader include.
NATURALITY_FOG_STEPS and NATURALITY_FOG_BORDER_START/FULL remain shader tuning
constants in assets/naturality/shaders/include/pixel_fog.glsl.
The override lives at assets/minecraft/shaders/include/fog.glsl and must be
reviewed when updating Minecraft. Resource packs can replace this include.
PixelFogGameTest captures blindness, underwater and lava scenes.

END FOG TRANSPARENCY
Normal End fog is now a screen-space blend toward the actual End sky,
including its current flashes. Only the render-distance start/end range controls
this fade; broad environmental haze no longer perforates nearby terrain.
Each configured pixel cell receives a continuous 0-100% transparency with a
smoothstep distance curve and subtle ordered variation, rather than binary
coverage/discard. The sky is captured before world geometry, then alpha-blended
over terrain and water using reconstructed opaque depth, before clouds. Near geometry and
unobstructed sky are preserved. Hands and GUI render afterward and are unaffected.
Terrain, water and other scene transparency are composited before edge fog;
clouds are held back until afterward and retain their own distance/alpha fade.
This preserves water's edge fade without applying distant terrain fog to nearby
clouds. Fog still uses the opaque depth behind water, as in the original scene
composite; it does not reconstruct a separate water-surface depth. The End reuses
the existing sky draw.
EndFogComposite owns one resize-aware sky texture, released on renderer close.
EndFogCompositeMixin captures the background after clear/sky passes (including
boss-suppressed sky), and blends after scene transparency closes, before clouds
and outlines. Cloud draw calls alone are deferred. Clouds use their sorted,
depth-tested alpha pass after fog in both scene transparency modes; terrain and
water retain the selected classic/OIT renderer. Both cloud layers are ordered
far-to-near, with no extra persistent targets. Opaque terrain continues to
occlude clouds. This separate cloud overlay does not share OIT sorting with
foreground translucent world geometry. Fog-disabled and obstructed-camera
modes retain the original integrated cloud OIT/classic path.
Pixel size and the custom-fog toggle continue to control this pass.
EndFogTransparency enables the End composite / Overworld atmosphere capture with an unobstructed camera;
water, lava, powder snow, blindness and darkness retain normal colored fog.
EndFogTransparencyMixin marks only the End's uploaded fog color alpha (-1 sentinel),
leaving the real FogData and sky clear color unchanged. Ordinary geometry shaders
skip colored fog in this mode. Binary End chunk-visibility dithering is removed.
PixelFogGameTest checks End/Overworld activation, blindness fallback, disabling
custom fog, resource reload and pixel size, with End stone panels against the sky.

OVERWORLD ATMOSPHERE FADE
Overworld terrain uses its material distance fog; water samples the current sky
and sunrise/sunset gradient in its own depth-tested material draw. There is no
Overworld fullscreen fog overlay after entities. OverworldFogAtmosphereMixin renders the vanilla sky
disc, sunset glow and below-horizon dark disc into the fog target, omitting the
sun, moon and stars. Thus opaque terrain continues to occlude celestial bodies
even at full fog opacity; open sky keeps its normal celestial rendering.
This adds a lightweight atmosphere draw, not a second world render. If sky is
suppressed, the normal cleared background is used. The existing Fog toggle and
pixel size apply. OverworldFogGameTest exercises sun/moon occlusion and captures
sunset comparisons as part of the focused fog suite.
The sky.vsh/sky.fsh overrides keep sky horizon blending independent of the
negative-alpha terrain-fog bypass. In custom atmosphere mode they compute sky
distance per fragment, then smoothstep from 16 to 80% of FogSkyEnd. This avoids
the fan's interpolated-distance facets and reaches the clear color before the
mesh edge, with a gentle zero-slope transition at both endpoints. The visible
sky and celestial-free backdrop share this gradient. Normal fog mode retains
the vanilla sky blend. Review both overrides when updating Minecraft shaders.

PORTAL OPENING ANIMATION
------------------------
Newly fire-lit portals use a server-authoritative opening sequence:
- 0-0.5 seconds: existing ignition fire fades to transparent; no portal blocks yet.
- 0.5-1.5 seconds: portal blocks exist in a temporary, non-traversable opening state.
  Actual animated portal texels use a brightness-dependent expo-in/linear/expo-out reveal, with lighter texels appearing
  faster; their original alpha is preserved. Glow and portal motes stay disabled.
- At 1.5 seconds: normal travel, glow and particles unlock. The portal, glow and
  mote palette receive a 2x brightness and 2x saturation pulse. The glow appears
  immediately at full opacity and 150% reach from the portal surface.
- 1.5-3.5 seconds: glow reach, brightness and saturation return to normal using expo-out.
Timing uses game ticks (20 per second); pausing the game pauses the sequence.
The opening state is transient server data, not a replacement block saved to disk.
A broken/obstructed frame cancels the transaction before it can overwrite blocks.
Existing portals and automatically generated destination portals are unaffected.
The server setting portalIntroAnimation controls new ignition sequences, independently of client visual settings. Portal block changes only controls portal emissivity.
For multiplayer, install Naturality on the server and clients to use the sequence;
a client connected to an unmodded server retains normal portal creation/travel.
Client visual settings: config/naturality.json. Server gameplay settings:
config/naturality-server.json (see below).

Implementation:
- naturality.portal.PortalOpeningManager: ignition delay, validation, travel lock,
  and synchronization with nearby clients (including new viewers mid-animation).
- PortalOpeningClient: per-frame animation with actual baked portal/fire models.
  It retains the completed custom surface while nearby to avoid a chunk-rebuild
  handoff flash; distant/unloaded portals return to normal terrain rendering.
- core/portal_opening.fsh: per-texel expo-in reveal and brightness pulse.
- core/fire_fade.fsh: alpha fade preserving the active fire resource-pack model.
- PortalOpeningTiming: duration/easing constants. FIRE_TICKS is the initial fade.
No animation assets need manual editing; the active resource pack supplies them.

Verification:
- gradlew build: geometry and opening timing/texel-order tests.
- gradlew runClientGameTest -PclientTests=portal: isolated integration world checks ignition, collision
  travel lock, readiness, interrupted Z-axis ignition and disabled-config fallback.
  Captures stages under build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots. Test code is excluded
  from the distributed mod JAR and normal runClient sessions.

END PORTAL SPATIAL PARALLAX
---------------------------
The End portal surface now samples 15 horizontal, upward-facing virtual texture
planes instead of projecting stars in screen space. Camera rays intersect each
plane, giving perspective foreshortening and position-dependent parallax. Layers
span 0.35 to 48 blocks behind the surface, with increasing gaps (depth fraction
raised to 1.65) so the deepest stars appear much smaller and farther away.
The dark sky backing uses direction-only
spherical mapping at infinity, with no finite-depth scaling or translation parallax.
Star layers composite far to near at 65% opacity, multiplied by texture alpha;
black pixels remain empty gaps. This gives a middle ground between the earlier
see-through effect and fully opaque stars. Brightness falls progressively from
100% on the nearest layer to 18% on the farthest (0.18 raised to layer fraction).
Nearest texel sampling preserves crisp pixel shapes. Palette hues remain,
normalized to remove the old additive
pass attenuation. Layer rotations and drifting animation remain. World-anchored
texture coordinates join across adjacent blocks.
The viewpoint comes from the inverse full projection/model-view transform,
including camera bob translations inside Minecraft's projection matrix, camera
rotation, third-person offsets and view effects. It is not the player's position.
This recreates the older spatial style rather than copying a specific old version.

Implementation: src/client/resources/assets/minecraft/shaders/core/
rendertype_end_portal.vsh and rendertype_end_portal.fsh. Tune depth, scale and
scrolling in portal_plane_uv and the fragment loop. The 16-layer End gateway
variant retains vanilla projection. The star planes add no geometry or gameplay

CLOUD LAYERS
------------
Mods > Naturality > Configure > Clouds controls two independent client cloud
layers. Cloud changes defaults ON; OFF restores the vanilla renderer. Minecraft's
Clouds: Off still hides both layers, and dimensions without clouds remain clear.
Defaults: open tops, eight square opacity rows over four-block-high sides, twelve
blocks per cloud texture cell. The top row is fully transparent. A small alternating
column variation makes individual opacity pixels visible rather than smooth bands.
Pixel sizes are world-space and move with clouds, not with the camera.
When side fading is enabled, all faces use the same unshaded cloud color;
directional surface shading is disabled. Day/night and weather tint still apply.
Disabling side fading restores vanilla directional face shading for that layer.
The upper layer is +40 blocks above the dimension's normal cloud height, offset
96 blocks on X and 48 on Z, and scrolls at 75% of vanilla speed. The base uses
normal cloud height and speed. Both sample the active resource-pack cloud texture.

Each layer has its own enabled toggle, Open top / Solid / Flat style, side fading
toggle, cell width (4-64 blocks), thickness (1-32), height offset (-256 to +256
relative to the dimension's normal cloud height), fade rows (2-32), X/Z offsets
(-512 to +512), and scroll speed (-200% to +200%; zero freezes movement).
Solid retains the lid; fading is independently controlled. Flat has no side faces,
so thickness/fading do not affect its appearance. Styles can be freely mixed.
Each setting has an individual Reset. Changes apply live and save on leaving the
page. JSON: clouds.enabled, clouds.base and clouds.upper in config/naturality.json.
Missing layers use defaults; invalid values clamp on load/save.

CloudRendererMixin shares vanilla's loaded cloud occupancy data with two compact
CloudLayerRenderer meshes, refreshed on resource reload and released on shutdown.
CloudPipelines and naturality core/clouds.vsh/fsh support classic and improved
transparency. Open shells have two-sided bottoms but no top
geometry. Gradient side walls show only their outward face; inward-facing fragments are discarded in every transparency pass. Bottom planes remain visible from both sides. Zero-alpha side pixels are discarded in all passes. Custom clouds first write depth for both layers without writing color, then
blend only the nearest surviving surface. Hidden sides/bottoms cannot stack
opacity through a nearer cloud. Both passes share the fade and face rejection,
so fully transparent pixels and inward gradient faces do not occlude anything.
The integrated OIT path copies scene depth once and shares cloud depth between
layers; it never resets depth between the two layer draws.
Classic transparency draws distant cloud cells/layers first; improved transparency
handles intersecting/translucent surfaces more accurately. Width scales the cloud
texture cells, not the full texture. Visible radius caps at 240 cells per layer to
stay within the compact signed coordinate format. Alternate renderers replacing
the vanilla cloud path may bypass this feature. Review hooks/shaders on upgrades.

SUN BEAMS
---------
Eight yellow beams draw immediately before the sun, anchored at its texture
center with cardinal/diagonal base angles. They rotate counterclockwise, averaging
one turn per five minutes, with smoothly varying speed that never reverses.
Independent eased length pulses take roughly 20-30 seconds with slower speed
modulation. Beams are straight constant-width rectangles (3.2 vanilla sun texels
wide). Their rotated silhouettes and tip fades are rasterized onto cells aligned
with the sun texture, using its actual sprite resolution on reload. The tip fade
reaches zero in texel-sized steps. Side edges have a one-texel coverage fade,
measured perpendicular to each rotating beam. Each cell retains uniform opacity
while smoothly fading in/out as the edge moves, avoiding full-pixel snapping.
Clear-weather root opacity is 10% at exact noon, easing to 60% at sunrise/sunset.
Vanilla rain fading multiplies this opacity.
View direction adds a 0.3x multiplier at least 45 degrees away from the sun,
easing smoothly to 1x when looking directly at its center. This uses the rendered
sun's view-space direction, so first/third-person camera rotation stays aligned.
Thus clear-weather noon opacity ranges from 3-10%, sunrise/sunset from 18-60%.
Color is sampled from the most yellow opaque texel of the active sun atlas sprite
(vanilla border #FFD54A), refreshed with the sprite on resource reload. Beams use
the sun's celestial OVERLAY blending, matching its tint against the sky. Sun and
moon textures are unchanged. Terrain/cloud occlusion follows normal sky ordering;
beams are excluded from the celestial-free atmosphere used for terrain edge fog.
The GPU rasterizes all eight beams on one static quad; buffers close with SkyRenderer.
GameTime drives movement, with integer cycles preserving continuity at its wrap.
Implementation: SunBeams, SunBeamsMixin, SunSpriteAccess and naturality shaders
core/sun_beams.vsh/fsh. Review hooks on Minecraft upgrades.

LIVE PROCEDURAL WATER AND LAVA
-----------------------------
Mods > Naturality > Configure > Custom Liquids has Water changes, Lava changes,
and Custom flowing lava, all ON by default and each with Reset. Changes apply
immediately without resource reload/chunk rebuild and save on Done/Escape.
JSON: "liquids": { "water": true, "lava": true, "flowingLava": true }.
Water OFF restores both water sprites from the active resource pack. Lava OFF
restores both lava sprites; the flowing-lava preference remains stored. With
Lava ON and Custom flowing lava OFF, still lava stays procedural and flowing
lava uses the active pack's ordinary sprite. Missing/null liquid settings default ON.

Terrain using minecraft:block/water_still, water_flow, lava_still and lava_flow
now renders from live cellular simulations instead of finite animation strips.
The Classic three-layer heat/wave equations are documented at:
https://github.com/ClassiCube/ClassiCube/wiki/Minecraft-Classic-lava-animation-algorithm
These were CPU algorithms, not historical GPU shaders. Naturality runs four
independently seeded 64x64 fields at client tick rate, with separate update buffers
and wrapped neighbors. Fields warm up for 160 steps and continue evolving without
a frame-loop reset. Flowing water uses a five-cell neighborhood along the flow
axis, forming longitudinal streaks rather than transverse bars. Flowing lava has
its own upstream-biased transport kernel with weak sideways diffusion, producing
elongated hot strands. Both also scroll the evolving field: one texel/tick for
water and one texel/two ticks for lava. These are directional extensions of the
linked equations, not exact ports of a particular historical flowing version.
Still lava uses shorter diffusion memory (9/11 retained neighborhood heat) and
a compensating local heat contribution, keeping smaller, distinct molten cells
without reducing average brightness. Still water retains the original stencil.

The terrain shader assembles four translated samples at each point using an
integer-hashed world lattice and smooth weights. Adjacent blocks therefore do not
reuse one visible tile. Heat is blended before applying the palette; pixels stay
on a 1/16-block surface grid. Vanilla UV derivatives supply the flow orientation.
Integer section origins plus local fractional coordinates retain precision at
negative coordinates and near the world border. Coplanar surfaces join across
block and chunk boundaries. Flowing top surfaces now use neighbor-aware flow
directions: FluidFlowMixin averages the four same-fluid cells touching each top
corner, and adjoining quads share those direction values. The shader interpolates
vectors (not wrapped angles) and evaluates the direction at each surface pixel.
This bends the flowing pattern continuously across direction changes. The old
resting-field edge blend is removed completely; flowing faces only sample their
own flowing simulation. Samples average same-height neighbors; height steps and
top/side projections can still change pattern at their junctions.
The ordinary opaque fluid vertex-alpha byte carries a quantized flow angle;
terrain.vsh restores its original opaque shade alpha even when custom liquids
are disabled. The paired mesh/shader changes must be kept together. No vertex
format expansion is needed. Corner calculations are cached per tessellated block
and use thread-local scoped state for concurrent section workers.
FluidFlowMixin also replaces vanilla's constant per-face light with per-vertex
trilinear samples of the world cell-centered sky/block light field. Solid samples
are excluded and the remaining weights normalized; fractional dynamic light is
preserved, and lava retains full block emission. Shared positions sample the same
field across blocks/sections, including slopes and side faces. The existing shader
then evaluates the interpolated light on the fixed 1/16-block pixel grid. This
lighting correction also applies with custom liquid textures disabled. The fluids
suite captures nighttime localized lighting over still and flowing water.
Lava's stochastic patches are now one block / 16x16 surface pixels across,
instead of two blocks, keeping motifs compact while varying across the world.
This is bounded stochastic texture
assembly, not an infinitely stored physical simulation or a mathematical guarantee
that no local pattern will ever recur. There is no short animation or spatial loop.

Lava takes its colors directly from the built-in vanilla still/flowing PNGs,
independently of resource packs. Every opaque source pixel is retained in a
256-wide lookup texture, with separate still/flowing regions and integer texelFetch.
There is no palette reduction to 256 samples, RGB interpolation, or invented
fallback color. Sorting by luminance retains frequency and all source colors.
The generated base color is an exact member of the corresponding vanilla palette;
normal scene lighting, shadow saturation and fog still modify final screen colors.
Heat uses a soft value/(value+0.35) mapping rather than clipping hot regions,
followed by a logistic palette-index remap (midpoint 0.46, slope factor 24) after
spatial blending. Unlike the former 0.30-0.62 smoothstep, this has no finite hot
cutoff selecting the rare brightest pixel across whole patches. Highlights retain
gradation and approach the source palette's extreme tail gradually. Inspection of
the bundled vanilla still animation found its brightest RGB FAEB72 occurs only
once; the next brightest F6DB66 also occurs once. These outliers must not be
treated as an ordinary hot-region color. The patterns still evolve live;
source animation frames are only scanned for color, never replayed. Water uses its Classic
green-channel intensity curve as grayscale, retaining modern biome tint, with
the Classic variable alpha curve. This intentionally differs from Classic's fixed
blue RGB. Existing lighting, fog, geometry, fluid physics and transparency remain.
Both regular and improved transparency use the same procedural color/alpha.
The four heat channels share one 64x64 RGBA texture with seven mip levels; no
per-block simulation, mesh rebuilding or additional geometry is needed.
Simulation pauses with the client, survives resource reloads, and is session-local.
Reload refreshes atlas bounds and lava palettes; GPU/NativeImage resources close
on client shutdown.

This applies to enabled named terrain sprites, including
resource packs replacing those IDs. Packs changing fluid material IDs, alternate
terrain renderers and shader replacements can bypass it. Water overlays against
glass/leaves, particles, item rendering and immersion overlays remain their usual
paths. No server changes or new texture PNG files are needed.

fluid_uniforms.glsl and procedural_fluids.glsl. Review pipeline/atlas hooks on

SURFACE-FITTED BLOCK FIRE
------------------------
Placed normal and soul fire use vanilla-exemplar synthesis. The former free-running
heat-to-palette mapping and artificial centered cone were rejected visually.
ProceduralFire now loads all 32 frames of all four built-in fire sprites into a
64x512 exemplar texture, preserving exact color, holes, bright pockets and tips.
A stable block-position seed selects animation phase; a lightweight 20 Hz counter provides the animation clock (a small clock/frame-metadata texture uploaded per tick). Local variation uses a fixed per-position cell
and direction, avoiding frame-to-frame random pixel popping. Vanilla variant phase
offsets are preserved; adjacent floor faces are inset by 1/1024 of their footprint
to avoid coincident faces and depth flicker. At most one of 256 canonical
cells can borrow a similar opaque neighbor (RGB distance below 0.18); every
canonical frame therefore retains at least 99.6% exact vanilla pixels and its
entire transparency mask. This intentionally uses vanilla animation exemplars,
rather than claiming the unconstrained heat simulation reproduces their look.
Updates pause with the game and survive resource reload. Built-in textures are
read at runtime; resource-pack recolors do not replace these exemplars.

Connected wall fire: ConnectedFire matches the four immediate neighbours of
each full-block, axis-aligned side sheet, requiring the same fire block and exact
matching support-plane geometry. A four-bit edge mask and bounded position seed
are encoded in vertex color. The shader quilts vanilla exemplars across connected
edges/corners with shared animation phases and static pixel selection, preserving
exact vanilla colors without temporal randomization or RGB crossfades. Interior
connections sample the middle of a flame instead of restarting at a white base
or transparent tip. Isolated sheets retain the original near-vanilla path. The
99.6% frame similarity bound above applies to isolated sprites, not quilted ones.
This uses no component traversal, extra geometry, textures or hitboxes; normal
neighbour chunk rebuilds update masks on placement/removal. Partial/rotated sheets,
floor and ceiling fire retain their individually fitted sprites. Position seeds
wrap every 32 blocks. The fire suite covers connected walls, gaps, detached fire,
neighbour removal, animation and resource reload, with connected-wall screenshots.

Fire is emissive; ordinary fog still applies. Physical face derivatives determine
a new raster at approximately 16 pixels per block. Every destination cell samples
the complete normalized exemplar domain, so a four-pixel post gets a four-pixel
raster of the whole flame, not a cropped strip or a squeezed sixteen-pixel grid.
Variation is bounded in canonical space; narrow rasters can amplify a changed cell.
Normal terrain and the portal ignition fade use the same simulation and shader.
Burning entities now use EntityFireRenderer: four double-sided, depth-writing
cutout sheets fitted to the interpolated entity bounding box, without camera
billboarding or vertically stacked repeating quads. Each side evaluates the full
procedural vanilla-exemplar flame domain at its physical pixel resolution. A
stable UUID-derived seed varies each side's animation. The root follows the box
bottom, horizontal clearance is 0.025 block, and the top extends 12% of hitbox
height (clamped to 0.15-0.35 block) above the box. entity_fire.fsh removes each
animation frame's empty headroom before rasterizing, keeping visible tips at the
overhang; the original irregular silhouette still animates.
Entity flame raster correction: reconstruct a premultiplied continuous color and
opacity field from vanilla exemplars before sampling the physical destination
grid. Extra animated detail is sampled at destination-cell density, equally in
X/Y, to avoid replicating source texels into tall rectangular bands. Opacity is
cut out and colors quantized per destination cell; no smoothing crosses that
grid. Reconstructed intermediate shades stay within the vanilla color range,
but do not have the isolated block sprite's exact-RGB/99.6% identity guarantee.
EntityFireState/Extract mixins retain actual X/Z extents and offsets as dimensions
change. EntityFireRender/Bindings mixins replace only FlameFeatureRenderer and
bind the shared fire textures. No entity collision, burning duration, or damage
changes. Flame particles and the first-person burning overlay keep their own paths.
The fire client suite includes burning zombies, pigs and slimes, bounding-box
extraction/overhang checks and front/oblique screenshots.

Shared main-side FireSurface/FireGeometry now define both rendering and hitboxes.
Slabs lower the flame root; posts, rails, gates, trapdoors, stairs and signs resize
the footprint. Standing sign boards retain their rotation. Open gate gaps stay
empty. Side fire moves into the neighboring support cell to touch the exposed
face, and matches that face's width/height, including all four fence-post sides.
Ceiling fire follows the supporting lower faces. Floor flame envelopes are 1.4

Side contact extends 1/16 block outward from the sheet. Rotated sign side fire uses one analytically rotated board sheet, never voxelized strips. Rotated selection/contact shapes use one conservative enclosing AABB, since Minecraft shapes cannot rotate. This trades a slightly broader diagonal hitbox for bounded geometry and query cost.
Fire still has no solid movement collision.

Fence/gate/sign dimensions use vanilla geometry; other supports use their outline

WATER MODEL INTERSECTION RIM
----------------------------
Water changes now includes a bright biome-water-colored rim where posed entity
models cross an exposed water top. It reuses PortalModelCapture and the portal's
nonzero-winding section rasterizer, on the same world-anchored 1/16-block X/Z
pixel grid as procedural water. The rim is inset by one pixel: the inner silhouette
boundary has 72% opacity, followed by two outer rows at 34% and 10%, then zero.
Deeper model interiors are excluded; unions of parts and nearby entities
share one pixel owner, preventing internal seams or doubled alpha. Local lightmap
lighting and fog apply; this is not an emissive light source.
Rim RGB matches the procedural water palette's brightest endpoint (intensity 1
times biome water tint), without an extra brightness multiplier or added white.

WaterIntersection captures each eligible model once per frame, then intersects it
with each water tile's two top triangle planes. Corner heights use vanilla's
neighbor weighting and top inset, including source/flowing water and negative
coordinates. The rim mesh follows those slopes with a 1/1024-block separation.
Slope classification is per pixel center, keeping each cell's opacity constant.
Only loaded exposed water tiles receive pixels. Dry ground, lava, covered tops,
fully submerged models and models wholly above the surface receive no rim.
The Water changes toggle controls the effect live, independently of portal glow.
Regular and improved transparency use matching depth-tested translucent layers.

Work is limited to 64 nearest candidate models within 32 blocks and the view
frustum; bounds larger than 16 blocks including the one-block search margin are
skipped. The search covers animated parts within that margin. Invisible entities,
spectators and the first-person camera entity are excluded. As with portal model
capture, texture-alpha holes, item submissions and custom non-Model renderers are
not traced; unsupported geometry gets no invented hitbox outline. No persistent
entity state, gameplay changes or new texture assets are involved. Alternate
renderers can bypass the particle submission hook.

Implementation: client/fluid/WaterIntersection, WaterIntersectionGeometry,
core/water_intersection.vsh/fsh and the existing particle-extraction mixin.
PortalModelCapture.mesh exposes origin-relative posed quads without changing the
portal-specific capture path. WaterIntersectionGeometryTest covers separate parts,
sloped planes, negative grid alignment, overlap and opacity falloff. The water
client suite checks intersecting/above/submerged pigs, the live toggle, boats at a
negative chunk boundary and screenshots in both transparency modes.
Validation: gradlew assemble checkPortalGlow; gradlew runClientGameTest -PclientTests=water.
Screenshots: build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots/*water-model-rim*.png.


RAIN AND DRIP WATER RINGS; CAULDRON SURFACES
------------------------------------------
Water changes includes expanding splash rings on sky-exposed water during rain.
Rings use the same world-anchored 1/16-block grid as the entity rim, with a hollow
pixel-circle core (65% opacity) and extra diagonal shoulder pixels (8% opacity).
The core is a one-pixel midpoint-circle line, with redundant solid corner elbows
removed; those corner pixels belong only to the faint shoulder. Radius advances
in whole-pixel steps, so intermediate animation frames cannot thicken the line.
Radius grows from one to four pixels over six ticks; the ring fades in over one
tick, fades out over its final three, and is removed after eight ticks (0.4 s).
Color uses the original brighter entity-outline formula: biome water tint * 1.25
plus 0.22 white, clamped per channel. The entity rim keeps its reduced brightness
and one-pixel inset. Both receive local light and ordinary fog.

WaterRipples samples up to 96 loaded columns per client tick within 24 blocks,
with rain-strength-scaled probability. The motion-blocking heightmap and vanilla
isRainingAt check exclude roofs, snow and rainless biomes. No chunks are loaded.
Actual falling water and dripstone-water particles also create a ring on their
first downward crossing, including sheltered water; they then stop at the surface.
WaterDropParticle impacts are included. Lava/honey/other drips are not converted.
The swept check uses the particle's collision-resolved movement, not a guessed
landing point. Hanging drips wait until their falling-particle stage.

WaterSurface supplies slope geometry and water-cauldron surfaces for BOTH the
model outline and splash rings. Cauldron levels 1/2/3 render at 9/12/15 sixteenths
of a block, with effects clipped to the inner 12x12-pixel bowl. Empty, lava and
powder-snow cauldrons are excluded. Rings on removed/drained/changed surfaces stop
rendering; dimension changes and disabling Water changes clear the tracker.
At most 512 rings are retained, and rendering is frustum culled. Animation pauses
with the client. Classic and improved transparency share the existing water-rim
layer, depth testing and lightmap. Resource packs remodeling cauldron interiors
may not match the vanilla bowl bounds used here.

Implementation: client/fluid/WaterSurface and WaterRipples, WaterIntersection,
WaterDripImpactMixin and WaterDropImpactMixin. No new texture assets or gameplay
changes. No extra framebuffers or terrain replays are added.
Validation: gradlew assemble checkPortalGlow; gradlew runClientGameTest
"-PclientTests=water-ripples,water". The focused water-ripples suite covers all
cauldron levels, empty/lava exclusion, actual falling-water/dripstone/rain providers,
sheltered drips, sky-only rain, expiry, disabling, and model rims in both
transparency modes. Screenshots: build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots/*water-ripples*.

WATERFALL SPLASH PARTICLES
-------------------------
Blocks and Effects > Liquids > Waterfall splash particles defaults ON, with an
individual Reset. JSON: liquids.waterfallParticles. Water changes must also be ON.
Small and big animated spray appears at the lower boundary where descending water
meets flat resting source water. The incoming tile may be a rendered slope,
including a source block whose top slopes near the waterfall. Its downhill edge
must face the receiving pool, on the same block level or one level below.
The receiving surface must be exposed and flat. Vertical-to-sloping transitions,
flowing-to-flowing transitions and runoff without a resting pool emit no spray.
This replaces the earlier upper-junction slope behavior. Ordinary flat water,
midair streams, cauldrons and dry tiles remain excluded. Cave waterfalls work
without sky exposure. Particles spawn at the receiving pool's surface height.

The supplied waterfall_0..11 and waterfall_big_0..11 texture sequences play once
at two ticks per frame (24 ticks / 1.2 seconds for the supplied assets). Big plumes
are 2.5x the nominal small size and comprise about 30% of births. Particles launch
upward and outward, expand slightly, decelerate and fade through the last 45% of
life. Both sprite sets retain their artwork with a pale biome-water tint and local
light. Ordinary particle collision and translucent rendering remain enabled.
Resource packs can replace the sprite lists. No generated textures are introduced.

WaterfallSplash scans 128 loaded columns per client tick in a 49x49x49 neighborhood
around the camera, refreshing in at most 19 ticks. Only borders within 24 blocks
can emit; up to 256 nearest borders and 48 births per tick cap dense scenes.
Every cached border is revalidated before spawning so source removal stops new
spray immediately. Existing particles finish their brief animation. Camera/world
changes refresh the cache; disabling clears it. Minecraft Minimal particles stops
spawning and Decreased halves the rate. No chunks load and fluid physics remain
unchanged. Cauldrons and dry receiving tiles remain excluded for this effect.

NaturalityParticles registers waterfall and waterfall_big particle types;
WaterfallParticle supplies the client providers and WaterfallSplash the detector.
Particle JSON lists are under assets/naturality/particles; source PNGs remain in
resources/textures/particle. Validation: gradlew assemble; gradlew runClientGameTest
-PclientTests=waterfall. The focused suite checks both providers, sizes, animation
lifetime, rising motion, real waterfall borders, no internal/underwater emission,
both transparency modes, toggling, Minimal particles and waterfall removal.
A second scene replaces the pool with a floor, asserting that sloping runoff
without a resting pool emits neither size. The pool scene explicitly rejects the
upper junction and accepts the next row's slope-to-resting-water boundary, with
screenshots in both transparency modes.

GREAT-HEIGHT WATER IMPACT AND SUBTLE WAKE (2026-09-29)
----------------------------------------------------
The great-height sound threshold is now strictly over five blocks. The same
entry creates a biome-colored rectangular splash column with temporary procedural
pixel-striped artwork. Footprint is 1.15 times entity bounding-box width/depth,
minimum 0.35 blocks. Height is 1.2 + 0.75*sqrt(fall-5), capped at seven blocks.
It starts at 8% height, reaches full height in four ticks, and collapses over
18 more ticks with a final fade. Up to 48 vanilla splash droplets launch upward
with speed scaled to column height. At most 64 columns are retained.
WaterImpactColumn shares the depth-tested water effect render layer, including
improved transparency. Existing waterfall artwork/tint remains unchanged.
Water rims vary opacity subtly every four ticks; vacated pixel cells leave a
six-tick wake at only 22% of rim opacity, fading to zero. Wake remains on the
water pixel grid and is reprojected onto the current water surface. Dry cells,
world changes and disabling the rim clear it. Cache is capped at 12,000 cells.
Splash column easing: rise uses expo-out over four ticks; collapse uses expo-in over eighteen ticks, with exact peak and expiry endpoints.
Splash revision: expo-out rise now takes one tick (0.05 s), followed by 18 ticks of expo-in collapse. Each side expands by up to 12 whole 1/16-block pixels during collapse. Footprint snaps to the water pixel grid; its perimeter uses the entity-rim 72/34/10% opacity rows with the brighter rain-ring palette, clipped to existing water surfaces.
Loud water splash audio now uses the shared positional_sound mono downmix cache, preserving sample rate/duration and handling stereo resource-pack replacements. sounds.json sets non-streaming playback and 16-block attenuation distance. The waterfall suite validates decoded mono channel count and preserved duration.
Splash rasterization revision: vertical geometry consists of complete 1/16-block rows anchored to the impact surface, including each jagged column top and a three-pixel white cap. Rise/fall reveal and remove rows rather than stretching eight bands. Width uses its own expo-out curve over the collapse duration, quantized to whole pixels, independently of the expo-in height.
Impact droplets alone opt out of WaterDropParticle lifetime expiry while airborne, preserving vanilla gravity, collisions and water/ripple impacts. Ordinary rain and other water droplets keep vanilla lifetimes.
Splash artwork revision: each geometry pixel re-samples a centered 32-cell virtual design as the footprint and height resize, rather than indexing stripes from a moving edge. It samples the live procedural water heat field used by terrain. Base RGB uses the terrain water curve (50+64*heat^2)/114 times biome tint; irregular upper foam blends to exactly the rain-ring palette (biome*1.25+0.22, clamped). Height, width and all color samples remain rasterized at 1/16 block.
Splash streak direction: artwork now samples the flowing-water field with its longitudinal axis aligned to splash height and a 3x vertical stretch. This replaces horizontal still-water bands with vertical streaks; palette, centered resampling and animation stay unchanged.
Splash timing adjustment: collapse shortened from 18 to 10 ticks (0.5 seconds), keeping expo-in height and independent expo-out widening. Total lifetime is now 11 ticks including the one-tick rise, with a three-tick final fade.
Splash rise is now 0.25 tick (12.5 ms). Both falling and widening share a clamped fall-progress clock that remains zero until this rise finishes; rise retains expo-out, then height uses expo-in and width independently uses expo-out over ten ticks.
Great-height water impacts now include AbstractBoat (boats, chest boats and rafts), with footprint derived from boat bounds. Mounted passengers remain excluded to prevent duplicate impacts.
Splash rise now lasts 1.5 ticks (six times the previous 0.25-tick rise). After the ten-tick collapse, the perimeter continues outward for ten additional ticks, advancing another twelve pixels per side while fading linearly to zero. Ring opacity is independent of the column fade so it persists continuously through collapse.
Splash width interpolation is now linear during collapse (still quantized to whole pixels). The residual ring also continues at the same constant 1.2 pixels per tick per side, so expansion speed remains continuous after the column disappears.
Splash falling duration doubled from 10 to 20 ticks (1 second). Linear widening spans the full descent; the 1.5-tick rise and subsequent ten-tick residual ring remain unchanged.

STACKABLE WEATHER CHANNELS (2026-09-29; storage updated 2026-09-30)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Weather appearance preferences remain under Mods > Naturality > Configure >
Lighting and Atmosphere > Weather. Weather pools and channel overrides are
managed by /weather <dimension> and saved in that world's naturality:weather
SavedData. They are not editable in the config screen or stored as global server
settings. Each dimension has automatic or independently overridden rain, wind,
temperature and direction channels. Nether/End pools remain disabled by default.

Rain landmarks: 0 clear, 10 drizzle, 25 ordinary rain, 50 vanilla thunderstorm,
100 extreme downpour. Rain also controls snowfall intensity. Sky/rain lighting
stays within vanilla 0-1 bounds. Precipitation is now clustered particles (see
below); above 50 the cluster budget rises cubically toward eight times normal density at 100.
WeatherState.windX()/windZ() controls rain angle and snow drift.

Wind is independent of rain. Leaf blocks and dry vegetation sway in the terrain
vertex shader; plant roots stay attached and tall plants share motion across
their halves. Sway samples a world-anchored 1/16-block grid and rounds horizontal
displacement to whole 1/16-block steps along world X/Z. This gives the motion a
pixel-stepped appearance independent of camera position, angle or resolution.
Only displacement is quantized, preserving original model placement and roots;
the geometry between vertices remains ordinary textured triangles. Very weak wind
can remain still until displacement reaches half a pixel. Wrapped periodic X/Z
phases retain precision at distant/negative coordinates and across chunk seams.
Ordinary solid terrain, fluids, fire and submerged foliage do not
sway. Vanilla and Fabric Indigo mesh paths carry dedicated tint-alpha markers;
texture tint/lighting/alpha are restored before shading. Resource packs or other
terrain renderers can replace/bypass these hooks. Wind loops use the supplied
resources/sounds/wind_weak.ogg and wind_strong.ogg, crossfaded from 30-80 strength.
Both are looping, camera-relative Weather-category audio, muted at zero wind,
attenuated under roofs and while immersed. They stop on disconnect/dimension
changes, and restart after sound reloads. Normal manual clients retain audio;
automated client tests keep their existing mute flag.

Temperature: 50 is the normal biome climate, 0 cold, 100 warm. Biomes without
precipitation (including deserts) remain dry. Naturally snowy precipitation,
including altitude-induced snow, remains snow regardless of the slider. Wet
biomes with base temperature below 0.95 can turn snowy when their base temperature
plus (slider-50)/40 falls below 0.15. Tropical biomes remain rainy. The same
world-aware query drives precipitation rendering, rain impact effects, server
snow accumulation, water freezing and block precipitation handling. Shared biome
instances never store mutable per-dimension temperatures. Vanilla light/support
rules and the snow accumulation gamerule still apply.

WeatherSystem owns server states keyed by dimension; clients receive snapshots
on join and every ten ticks, clear them on disconnect and select the current
dimension explicitly. Unmodded servers retain vanilla weather. Install Naturality
on both sides for synchronized custom weather. Overworld is enabled by default;
Nether and End have separate reserved profiles, both disabled. Unknown dimension
IDs default disabled and can have their own JSON profiles. Nether/End-specific
phenomena and precipitation renderers are not implemented yet.

Automatic Overworld precipitation retains vanilla's wet/dry scheduling and sleeping.
The /weather command now edits independent slider overrides (see below).
During wet periods a smooth seeded curve supplies 5-49
rain intensity, or 50-100 during thunder periods. Independent smooth curves drive
wind (default range 0-100) and temperature (15-75), with per-dimension seeds.
New worlds start with automatic temperature exactly 50 for two Minecraft days.
Temperature follows a 12-day seasonal cycle with two-day plateaus and one-day
transitions (see SEASONAL TEMPERATURE below). Manual temperature overrides still
apply, including deliberately cold values. Disabling advance_weather holds the
automatic climate at its current value.
Each profile can set minWind/maxWind and minTemperature/maxTemperature in JSON.
The advance_weather gamerule freezes automatic curves; manual overrides and
/weather slider commands still work. Rain/wind approach targets by 0.5 points per tick;
automatic temperature by at most 0.005; manual temperature by 0.25. Overrides persist in
server config; automatic curves restart from world game time when loading a world.

WEATHER SLIDER COMMANDS (2026-09-29)
-----------------------------------
The vanilla /weather command tree is replaced with server-owned slider controls:
  /weather <dimension> rain <0-100|auto>
  /weather <dimension> wind <0-100|auto>
  /weather <dimension> temperature <0-100|auto>
  /weather <dimension> direction <0-360|auto>
  /weather <dimension> auto
  /weather <dimension> status
  /weather <dimension>             (list the pool's sliders and their settings)

Examples:
  /weather minecraft:overworld rain 10
  /weather minecraft:overworld wind 75
  /weather minecraft:overworld temperature 20
  /weather minecraft:overworld direction 270
  /weather minecraft:the_nether wind 90
  /weather minecraft:the_end temperature 5
  /weather minecraft:overworld rain auto

Numbers are integer slider targets, never durations. A number enables that
channel's override; auto releases it without changing the other channels. Direction is in degrees and wraps around at 360.
/weather <dimension> auto releases all four overrides for the selected dimension.
The dimension is mandatory and must come first, even in /execute contexts and
from the console. Tab completion after /weather lists dimension IDs; after a
dimension it lists that pool's sliders (rain, wind, temperature and direction),
plus auto/status. Entering just /weather <dimension> displays the pool's settings.
Dimension IDs must identify a loaded server dimension. For the selected dimension,
rain 0 clears precipitation, rain 25 gives ordinary rain and rain 50 a thunderstorm.
The previous slider-first/trailing-dimension syntax is rejected. The old
clear/rain/thunder preset commands and duration arguments are no longer registered.

Commands require the same gamemaster permission as vanilla /weather. Targets and
override flags persist in naturality-server.json and are shared with the settings
menu. Existing smooth transitions and client synchronization apply. Setting a
Nether/End slider saves its own pool without enabling weather there; feedback
explicitly says that the dimension is disabled. Status distinguishes configured
override targets from current transitioning values. Status never mutates config.
Unknown/custom loaded dimensions get their own disabled profile on first edit.

Implementation: WeatherCommands plus WeatherCommandMixin replaces vanilla
registration before it creates incompatible preset/duration branches. Integration
tests: gradlew assemble runClientGameTest -PclientTests=weather-commands,weather.

SKY EVENTS / OVERWORLD METEOR SHOWER (2026-09-30)
------------------------------------------------
Sky Events has explicit per-dimension event pools; Rainbow additionally requires
a moderate rain value.
The Overworld contains Meteor Shower, Aurora Borealis and Rainbow; the End
contains End Flashes. The Nether has none. The server evolves and synchronizes each event's
0-20 strength to compatible clients on join and every ten ticks. Both server
and client need Naturality for automatic events; unmodded servers send no events.
Individual meteor positions are client-local cosmetic randomness.

Use the operator command `/skyevent <dimension> <event> <strength>` to set a
persistent strength override from 0 to 20. For example,
`/skyevent minecraft:overworld meteor_shower 12`. Event names are suggested
for the selected dimension. Run `/skyevent <dimension> <event> auto` to resume
automatic scheduling. The command is disabled for non-operators. Overrides are
saved with the world, with independent settings for each dimension. Missing
settings receive defaults; strengths clamp to 0-20 on load/save.

Automatic meteor showers roll to start each eligible night tick (10% chance
over one minute while quiet) and remain active for 12,000-24,000 ticks.
Active strength eases toward a new random
1-20 target every 100-600 ticks; inactive strength eases to zero. The
advance_weather gamerule freezes automatic evolution. Manual overrides still
apply. These first-version event clocks are session state: restarting a server
begins quiet. Manual settings are saved separately from clocks.

Strength controls spawning across the sky hemisphere, from zero to ten meteors
per second at 20, capped at 128 live instances. Meteors use a camera-centered sky
sphere, moving down-left along their tilted head direction. Great-circle motion
keeps orientation aligned with velocity near the zenith. Camera rotation projects
these world sky directions naturally; they are not screen-fixed particles.
Animation uses resources/textures/sky/meteor_shower_0.png through _6.png.
Each starts randomly on 0, 1 or 2, fades in over six ticks, and flickers between
that frame and its successor every two ticks for a chosen 20-80-tick duration.
It then plays each remaining frame once for two ticks and is removed. There is
no lifetime opacity fade-out. Meteor showers are night-only; vanilla rain brightness
modulates the celestial draw just as it does the sun/moon.

Rendering reuses vanilla CELESTIAL blending, so black texture pixels merge into
the sky. It draws within the regular sky pass, before terrain and clouds, and is
excluded from the celestial-free terrain-fog backdrop. Frame textures load before
the render pass; a single static quad is shared by every instance and released
with SkyRenderer. World changes/disconnects clear sprites and spawning remainder;
disconnect also clears synchronized strength. Resource packs can replace frames.

Implementation: naturality.sky (type pools, per-world settings, cycle, payload and command),
client.sky.MeteorShower and SkyEventsMixin.
Focused validation: gradlew assemble runClientGameTest -PclientTests=sky-events.

------------------------------------
The Overworld event pool now also contains aurora_borealis. Aurora can start and
render during both daytime and nighttime. Meteor Shower remains night-only.
Use /skyevent minecraft:overworld aurora_borealis 20 to preview full strength,
0 to fade it away, and auto to restore natural occurrence. Overrides persist
in that world's saved sky-event settings, independently per dimension.
Missing aurora settings are added automatically when existing configs load.

Natural auroras are local to occupied 1024x1024-block regions. Players sharing a
region share one event cycle and one random attempt, so player count does not
multiply its frequency. The coldest occupied biome in the region supplies its
biome chance. Biome base temperature and natural snowy precipitation contribute
independently of the custom weather temperature; disabled custom weather uses 50.
Every 600 eligible ticks, the base 0.15% start chance is multiplied by up to 9
for cold/snowy biomes, and by 2^((50-temperature)/32) for custom weather. Thus
hot weather reduces chance, cold weather increases it, and both sources of cold
combine. Approximate per-attempt chances: mild biome/neutral weather 0.15%,
snowy biome with base temperature 0/neutral weather 1.31%, the same snowy biome
at weather temperature 0 about 3.86%. No biome is categorically excluded.

Active cycles last 2400-7200 ticks, easing toward random strengths 4-20 every
200-600 ticks, followed by a 2400-tick retry cooldown. The advance_weather rule
freezes automatic evolution. Unoccupied regions do not accumulate events and
are forgotten after 1200 ticks. Event clocks are session-local, as with the
initial Meteor Shower implementation; manual command overrides are saved.
AuroraEvents handles regional evolution; AuroraCycle isolates the probability
and timing calculations. SkyEventsClient receives all event channels through
one shared network receiver; Meteor Shower retains its own sprite lifecycle.

DEFAULT VISUAL STYLE
AuroraGeometry creates stable, world-anchored Catmull-Rom curtain paths, sampled
into broad connected flat panels. Three main ribbons per 1024-block tile each
have a branch that converges onto the main spline with a shared endpoint and
tangent. Panels share exact endpoints to avoid cracks. The lower edges sit
roughly at Y=370-486, with 112-160-block-high curtains. These are intangible
render geometry: flying above, beneath or through them gives real parallax and
places the camera on the corresponding side. No blocks/entities/collisions exist.
The alternate style in the sketch is reserved for a future implementation;
only the requested faceted curtain style is currently rendered.

The renderer caches only the nine nearby world tiles and releases abandoned
tiles, world state and GPU buffers on dimension/disconnect/renderer shutdown.
Coordinates are translated relative to the camera in double precision before
conversion to render floats, so the world pattern does not slide with the camera.
The shader fades by distance across 650-1000 blocks and extends atmospheric
geometry past terrain's far clip plane while retaining foreground depth tests.

resources/textures/sky/aurora.png is sampled directly as vertical color ramps:
columns are different palettes; rows go from dark at the top to light at the
bottom. The supplied texture is 2x5. Resource packs may replace it with other
palette dimensions. All rows are used and adjacent palettes transition along
the curtains. The aurora shader animates irregular vertical highlights, a bright
lower fringe, and varying upper reach on fixed 3x3-world-unit texture cells.
The grid remains fixed while the values animate, retaining a pixelated texture.
There is no painted aurora bitmap or pre-generated animation to regenerate.
Strength 0-20 controls both overall opacity and the number of ribbon families;
new families appear gradually as strength rises. Local transitions ease over
up to four seconds. Rain dims the aurora through the normal rain-brightness value.

AuroraRenderer draws emissive, two-sided curtains with celestial additive
blending and a read-only scene depth test, after scene fog and before the custom
cloud overlay. Foreground terrain occludes it and ordinary clouds cover it.
It supports both classic and improved scene transparency. Underwater/lava,
powder-snow, blindness and darkness views suppress the draw. Its world pass is
independent of whether ordinary clouds are enabled. Resource reload resolves
the current palette again before opening its render pass.

END STARFALL WEATHER (2026-10-06)
--------------------------------
Starfall is implemented as an End weather slider, controlled with
/weather minecraft:the_end starfall <0-100|auto>. Values ease by 0.5 per tick.
Automatic showers have their own activation, duration and retarget clocks,
with a new-world quiet period. Strength, target, overrides, timers and random
sequence are saved with End weather. Legacy reserved starfall integer values
migrate into the new versioned saved state. The End status lists starfall's mode.
StarfallWeather spawns in loaded columns around non-spectator End players.
Streams start at Y=144 and Y=-24 and aim through the general island band at Y=64,
with slight horizontal inclination and speed 1.4-2.2 blocks/tick. Intensity
increases shower density; nearby players share their local shower. At most 64
stars/fizzles occupy a local 112-block-radius volume, including below-island
space. No absent chunk is loaded/generated for a spawn or a projectile step.
Two transient projectile entity types are registered: naturality:falling_star
and naturality:fizzle. Their swept ray checks hit terrain and entities without
skipping fast impacts. Real stars apply 24 direct-hit damage plus a power-2.5
explosion with ExplosionInteraction.NONE (no terrain destruction or ignition).
Fizzles burst into temporary END_ROD/POOF placeholders after 8-27 ticks or before
any imminent collision; they never damage entities or explode. Stars expire
after 180 ticks or when moving into unavailable chunks. They ignore gravity.
Both use the supplied resources/entity models/star.java geometry and
resources/textures/entity/star.png, centered on the star. The two model cubes
rotate forward at 9 degrees/tick and left at 6 degrees/tick. UVs are static;
there is no scrolling fireball shell pass.
The registered naturality:star_trail particle uses the supplied replaceable
resources/textures/particle/star_trail.png. One particle remains attached to the
rendered star's center. Its local Y axis follows opposite velocity so the trail
extends behind travel; roll alone adjusts toward camera position. Its anchor is
76% down the 6-block card, below the image center. The trail interpolates the
same entity position as the model, stays independent of model spinning, is
full-bright and wind immune, and vanishes when the tracked star vanishes.
Validation: offline assemble plus starfall, weather-commands and end-weather
client integration suites passed (build/starfall-final.log). Checks include
upward/downward block impacts, unchanged island terrain, direct damage,
harmless timed/collision fizzles, inactive weather, bounded bidirectional
showers, both renderer registrations, attached wind-immune trails and saved
weather sequence. Screenshot inspected:
build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots/0000_starfall-models-and-trails.png.

LIRESTONE PATCHES AND BOULDERS (2026-10-06)
----------------------------------------
LirestonePatches adds a continuous, seed-derived 3D deposit field to the existing
EndStoneLayers surface pass. It replaces both the regular End Stone cap and
Smooth End Stone interior, without filling air or replacing unrelated blocks.
Lirestone counts as part of the same rock layer, so patches do not reset the
two-block End Stone cap. Broad noise uses 28-block horizontal / 21-block vertical
scales; finer boundary detail uses 9/8-block scales. Fine noise is skipped away
from deposit edges. RandomState's named positional random factory supplies the
seed through the supported API. World coordinates keep patches continuous across
chunk borders; the pass reads and writes only its own chunk.

LirestoneBoulderFeature is registered through Fabric's biome modification API
in End biomes at LOCAL_MODIFICATIONS, before vegetation. The placed feature is
src/main/resources/data/naturality/worldgen/placed_feature/lirestone_boulder.json:
rarity 1/10 attempts per chunk, in-square placement, WORLD_SURFACE_WG heightmap,
and biome filter. Void, steep or obstructed sites reject the attempt. Two offset
lobes make small, partly buried boulders with radii 2-3 and nominal exposed
heights 2-4 blocks. Every occupied column requires rock immediately beneath its
lowest block. The entire footprint is validated before any writes, and only air,
End Stone, Smooth End Stone or Lirestone can be replaced. Boulders may cross
chunk borders normally as a worldgen feature. Added geometry is restricted to
the End beyond radius 1280; material deposits also occur on the central island.
Existing Lirestone chorus-support tags, mining properties and crafting remain.

Validation: offline assemble and lirestone-worldgen, smooth-endstone and
end-chunk-performance passed without the old test-isolation script:
build/lirestone-worldgen-checks.log. Tests cover seed reproducibility/variation,
coherent deposits, replacement of both source layers, preservation of unrelated
blocks/air, actual initial generation, sparse boulders, supported cross-chunk
placement and atomic rejection of obstructed/unsupported attempts. The fresh
256-chunk survey has 161,250 Lirestone blocks out of 1,406,374 natural rock blocks
(11.5%), 2,944 patch-covered surface columns, and 93 exposed boulder columns out
of 27,578 land columns (0.34%); ten chunks contain boulder tops. Both screenshots
were inspected: 0000_lirestone-patches.png and 0001_lirestone-boulder.png under
build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots. The terrain density/solid fingerprints are
unchanged. The 64-chunk gateway workload remains about 490 ms; the three outer
64-chunk workloads take 491-662 ms. Profile retained as build/lirestone-worldgen-profile.txt
and .jfr. Production JAR rebuilt at build/libs/naturality-1.0.0.jar.
Restart Minecraft and generate fresh chunks to see these features; existing
terrain is not rewritten.

REGIONAL LIRESTONE AND REDUCED CLIFF PATCHES (2026-10-06)
------------------------------------------------------
LirestonePatches now samples a separate seed-derived regional field at a
1600-block scale. Quintic easing creates broad areas with exactly zero patch
abundance and gradual transitions toward richer areas. Its value is calculated
once per column. Zero-abundance columns skip deposit noise entirely; other
columns raise the deposit threshold as abundance falls, so patches fade naturally.

Deposits favor the upper three blocks of each contiguous rock run. Their threshold
increases smoothly over depths 4-15, retaining only occasional deep deposits.
This greatly reduces patches exposed along cliffs and undersides without reading
neighboring chunks or trimming existing terrain. Patches still replace both End
Stone and Smooth End Stone. Lirestone boulder frequency and placement are unchanged.

RISE WEATHER AND DISABLED NATURAL STARFALL (2026-10-06)
----------------------------------------------------
Automatic starfall activation is temporarily disabled. Existing natural event
clocks are cleared, and restoring an automatic starfall state starts calm.
Manual starfall overrides still work; starfall auto eases back to zero even when
advance_weather is paused. No shared config setting is introduced.
Rise is an End weather slider: /weather minecraft:the_end rise <0-100|auto>.
It has automatic activation, duration and retarget clocks after the new-world
quiet period. Overrides and strength ease at 0.5/tick. Rise values and clocks are
saved with per-world End weather and transmitted in EndWeatherPayload; command
status/completions and the all-auto command include rise.
RiseWeather generates a bounded client particle volume around the player in the
End. Cards start at Y=-8 to -16, rise at 0.35-0.75 blocks/tick with a small lateral
velocity, fade at arrival/expiry, and stop at collidable terrain. Spawn columns
must already be loaded. Population is capped at 600; strength controls spawn
rate. Weather motion is wind immune and clears its population on world changes.
Each card is a plain, uniformly colored square with its transverse axis squashed
to 1/(1+verticalSpeed*5) of its length. The long axis is locked to velocity using
the same PortalMoteGeometry orientation as Nether portal particles. Roll alone
adjusts the plane toward camera position. RiseRenderLayer handles both ordinary
translucency and OIT, preserving terrain depth/fog occlusion.
The shader samples textures/sky/aurora_end.png directly, without fabricated
colors or a separate texture copy. A looping phase cycles through all palette
columns over 200 ticks with smoothstep blending (including last-to-first).
An independently phased sine pulse moves through all brightness rows, also
interpolating adjacent rows. A 15-bit brightness coordinate avoids coarse
8-bit pulse stepping. Render partial ticks interpolate both color and brightness.
RiseGameTest checks natural starfall stays off across 150000 ticks, manual
starfall remains available, rise commands/network state, saved clock replay,
upward motion, velocity alignment, width, palette-coordinate motion, terrain
contact and bounded population. The visual fixture clears an observation space
so the test camera is not inside End stone; screenshot inspected:
build/run/clientGameTest/screenshots/0000_rise-aurora-colored-rectangles.png.
Validation: assemble and isolated Rise/End-weather/starfall integration suites passed (build/rise-complete.log). Weather-command checks passed in build/rise-final.log before the unrelated shared-build class deletion. Shader and color-rectangle screenshot visually inspected.

LIRESTONE LEDGE RIMS AND COMPLETE SMALL ISLANDS (2026-10-06)
----------------------------------------------------------
Ordinary Lirestone patches now favor the rims of flat ledges. EndTerrainDensity
exposes material information from its cached horizontal slices: near-surface
ribs with gentle slopes supply an edge affinity, fading between 6 and 18 blocks
inside the outline. Overlapping slices at the same elevation account for the
union of nearby landings, avoiding artificial rims between joined pieces.
EndStoneLayers uses that affinity once per exposed rock run and raises the patch
threshold toward flat interiors. The existing regional absence and depth-based
cliff reduction still apply. Boulders are five times more likely to proceed near
a terrain drop within eight blocks than on an interior flat surface; their full
foundation and collision checks still prevent hanging or destructive placement.

The generator's small satellite outcrops have an independent 1/20 material
selection chance. Only genuinely detached outcrops are eligible: conservative
capsules around their finite rock spines include erosion and block-adjacency
clearance and must not intersect any neighboring formation. These checks use
seeded geometry, never loading neighbor chunks to identify the island. Entire
conversion also requires positive regional abundance throughout the outcrop's
full bounding rectangle, preserving patch-free regions. The whole outcrop's
regular cap and smooth body become Lirestone, consistently across chunk borders.
The protected central/transition region is excluded. Selection uses a separate
random stream so geometry does not change. Bounded per-thread metadata caches
retain no chunk contexts; comparisons use grid coordinates rather than cached
object identity to remain independent of cache eviction and generation order.

Validation: offline assemble plus lirestone-worldgen, smooth-endstone and
end-chunk-performance passed in build/lirestone-rims-islands.log (1m16s).
A focused repeat using independently measured physical terrain drops passed in

patch-covered rim columns out of 12,453 (8.85%), versus 123 of 7,518 flat-interior
columns (1.64%): a 5.4x preference. Ordinary deep cliff patches remain at 497 of
53,557 side blocks (0.93%). Rare whole-island conversions are excluded from that
ordinary-patch comparison.
